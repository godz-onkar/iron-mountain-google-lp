Fonts and colors

  • Headings, buttons, labels: Poppins 600 to 700 (500 for the second half of the headline). Body: Newsreader 400, 18 px on desktop. Both are Google Fonts. The logo’s Nexa font is licensed; use it for headings only if Iron Mountain holds the license.
  • Colors: background #F8F6F2, alternate band #E9E1D5, headings #26211F, body #4B4440, small text #6E6560 (#615954 on the alternate band), red #8B0507 (hover #6E0405), stars #C98D00, ridge line #5D6066.
  • Section rhythm: no two neighboring sections share a background. Hero off-white · When it’s time band · Meet off-white · Reviews band · What he repairs off-white · How he gets you back band · How long until you’re back dark · Your first visit off-white · FAQ band · Close off-white · Footer band. No divider lines between sections; the color change does that job.
  • Section marker: a small mountain-peak line (56 × 12 px, #5D6066, 1.5 px stroke) sits 20 px above every section heading, the same shape as the peak in the close.
  • Red means act: red appears only on Request buttons and links. Call buttons are outlined, so the eye learns the main action.
  • Corners: buttons 6 px, photos and video tiles 4 px, the desktop hero portrait 24 px.
  • Real photos only. The anatomical illustration on today’s page goes. The site’s ROSA robot photos stay out (that system is mostly for knee replacements, which he doesn’t do).

Layout, section by section

Content max width 1200 px; side padding 20 to 40 px.

  1. Header (sticky). Logo left; phone number with “Weekdays, 8 to 4” and the Request button right. Phone: logo and tap-to-call number only.
  2. Hero. Text left: small line, the two-size headline, subhead, Request and Call, the hours line. Dr. Ayzenberg’s portrait right, 340 px wide with rounded corners, its bottom level with the buttons. The headline’s first half is large (about 50 px); “then plans your surgery and recovery around your life” sits on its own line, smaller (about 28 px). Phone: the portrait goes under the buttons at full width, so both buttons show on the first screen of a 390 px phone. Trust bar under the hero (one row on desktop, 2 by 2 on the phone).
  3. When it’s time to get it fixed. Heading and lead left; the five-line checklist right with thin dividing lines; the “Not sure yet” line under it. No button here on purpose.
  4. Meet Dr. Ayzenberg. The 6,000-mile video left (the second photo on the knee page); the three paragraphs and the Request button right.
  5. Reviews. Band background. Peter T.’s review set large as the lead, with the Google rating beside it; three more reviews in a row; then the page’s patient videos as click-to-play tiles; then a Request button (on all five pages, so the reader can act right after the proof). Three patient reviews to one physical therapist on every page.
  6. What he repairs. Three tight columns (the page’s joint first) under a heavy rule, then “He doesn’t do knee or hip replacements.”
  7. How Dr. Ayzenberg gets you back. Four numbered rows (01 to 04) with thin dividing lines, not cards.
  8. How long until you’re back. Dark background. The recovery figures sit on a line that climbs left to right with a dot over each figure (the mountain motif); on the phone it becomes a vertical line with three dots. Request button at the end. If GHL can’t build the line, put the three figures in a row on the dark background.
  9. Your first visit. The “you’ll leave with” list left; the three numbered steps, the coverage line, and Request plus Call right.
  10. Questions before you book. Six questions as an accordion, the first open.
  11. Close. Office photo; “See the surgeon who’ll do your surgery”; Request and Call; then name, address, hours, phone and a Google Maps link, matching the Google Business Profile exactly.
  12. Footer. Logo, copyright, privacy policy and the in-person exam line.
  13. Mobile bar. Call and Request My Visit, fixed to the bottom once the hero buttons scroll out of view. Phone only.
